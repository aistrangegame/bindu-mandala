# The Homes — Śakti Cards, Rings 1–2

*Raw material for Claude Design. Every field is real, from the Airtable base. Nothing here is a description of the data — this is the data.*

Field key: **Quality** = the room's soul · **Function** = what the room does to you · **Somatic** = the room's physics · **Location** = altitude and gravity · **Tattva** = element modifier · **Bīja** = tuning · **Etymology** = threshold ritual · **Phrase** = inscription over the door · **Iconography** = her form, posture, attribute, color.

# RING 1 — Trailokyamohana (28) — Bhūpura · Topaz on lymph · Day–night clock · Feet · earth

## Siddhis (1–10)

**1 · Aṇimā · अणिमा**
Quality: Smallness · Tattva: Aṇu (the atomic) · Location: Bindu point at crown / single point of attention
Function: Grants entry into any place no matter how confined. Without Her, no atomic-scale recognition is possible.
Somatic: Awareness that becomes smaller than thought, smaller than breath, smaller than the gap between two sensations.
Etymology: Aṇu (atom / minute particle) + suffix — 'atomic-ness,' the power to become atomic.
Phrase: Thank you for the smallness that contains all.
Iconography: A luminous goddess fine as a dust-mote, pale and near-translucent, holding a noose to draw the vast into the tiny. The power to shrink awareness to a single point and pass through the smallest door. The body knows her as focus.

**2 · Mahimā · महिमा**
Quality: Vastness · Tattva: Mahat (the vast) · Location: Periphery / horizon of attention
Function: Holds the polarity of small/large with Aṇimā. Vastness is mastery of unrestricted expansion.
Somatic: Awareness suddenly opening beyond the skin, the room, the city — into space itself.
Etymology: Mahā (great / vast) + suffix — 'great-ness,' the state of vastness.
Phrase: Thank you for the vastness that holds all.
Iconography: An expansive radiant form, golden and immense, arms open to the horizon, a goad in hand. The power to grow without limit and fill all space. The body knows her as the breath that expands the chest until the room feels small.

**3 · Laghimā · लघिमा**
Quality: Lightness · Tattva: Vāyu (air / movement) · Location: Solar plexus rising upward
Function: Removes the burden of density. Things become easier when She is present.
Somatic: Sudden buoyancy. A smile arriving unbidden. The body remembering it is not made only of weight.
Etymology: Laghu (light / not heavy) + suffix — 'light-ness,' the state of weightlessness.
Phrase: Thank you for the lightness that lets me move.
Iconography: Weightless and silver-bright, half-dissolved into air, her garments lifting though there is no wind. The power to become light and rise. The body knows her as the lift that comes the moment a burden is set down.

**4 · Garimā · गरिमा**
Quality: Weightedness · Tattva: Pṛthvī (earth) · Location: Mūlādhāra / sit-bones / soles
Function: Stability at the cosmic level — the capacity to be present without flinching.
Somatic: Body settling into the chair. Feet finding the floor. Breath dropping into the belly. The arrival of staying.
Etymology: Guru (heavy / weighty) + suffix — 'heavy-ness,' the state of immovable weight.
Phrase: Thank you for the weight that holds me here.
Iconography: Dark and deep-rooted, heavy as mountain stone, seated immovable with one palm pressing the earth. The power to become unliftable. The body knows her as the settledness no argument can shift.

**5 · Īśitva · ईशित्व**
Quality: Sovereignty · Tattva: Īśvara-tattva (lord-principle) · Location: Crown and spine alignment
Function: Confers the dignity that doesn't need permission. Mastery of one's own kingdom (which is, ultimately, everything).
Somatic: Posture organizing itself upward. The spine remembering its line. The gaze becoming level.
Etymology: Īśa (lord / ruler) + tva (state-of-being) — 'lord-ship,' the state of being a lord.
Phrase: Thank you for the sovereignty that knows itself.
Iconography: Crowned and erect, a sceptre in hand, gaze level and unhurried. Lordship — the power to govern what arises. The body knows her as the quiet authority of the one in the room who need not raise her voice.

**6 · Vaśitva · वशित्व**
Quality: Mastery · Tattva: Saṃyama (integration) · Location: Throat / lungs
Function: The capacity to influence experience by being utterly aligned. What is integrated radiates.
Somatic: Breath under control, then breathing on its own. Mind under control, then quieting on its own.
Etymology: Vaśa (under-control / subdued) + tva — 'control-ship,' the state of having mastery.
Phrase: Thank you for the mastery that comes from being mastered.
Iconography: Holding a noose lightly, half-smiling, the elements leaning toward her of their own accord. The power by which things yield without being forced. The body knows her as the moment desire and circumstance quietly align.

**7 · Prākāmya · प्राकाम्य**
Quality: Irresistible Will · Tattva: Saṅkalpa (resolute intention) · Location: Solar plexus / hands
Function: The vector quality of consciousness — the directional force without resistance.
Somatic: Movement happening before deliberation. The body knowing what to do before the mind has decided.
Etymology: Pra (forth / forward) + kāma (desire) + ya — 'forward-desire,' will that is not resisted.
Phrase: Thank you for the will that flows.
Iconography: Bow drawn, flame-coloured, the will already become fact. The power whose wish meets no resistance. The body knows her as the rare clarity in which intention and outcome are a single motion.

**8 · Bhukti · भुक्ति**
Quality: Enjoyment · Tattva: Rasa (essential taste) · Location: Mouth / belly / whole body
Function: The receptive capacity — to fully receive and taste what is here without rejection.
Somatic: Mouth softening. Eyes drinking the light. Skin receiving warmth without holding back.
Etymology: Bhuj (to enjoy, eat, experience) + ti (action-noun) — 'enjoyment / full experience.'
Phrase: Thank you for the enjoyment that includes everything.
Iconography: Soft and replete, a brimming cup in hand, lips touched with nectar. The power to truly taste what is given, to receive pleasure fully. The body knows her as the sigh of genuine satisfaction.

**9 · Icchā · इच्छा**
Quality: Willingness · Tattva: Icchā-śakti (will-power, the first of the three) · Location: Heart / first impulse
Function: The originating impulse. Before knowing, before action — the wanting itself.
Somatic: The first stirring. The lean-toward. The orientation arising before any specific aim.
Etymology: Iṣ (to wish, desire) → icchā = 'willing, the act of wanting.'
Phrase: Thank you for the willing that begins everything.
Iconography: Leaning gently forward, a half-open lotus in hand, the first stirring of yes. Desire at its purest — the wish before the grasping. The body knows her as the soft opening toward something before the mind decides.

**10 · Prāpti · प्राप्ति**
Quality: Reach · Tattva: Pūrṇatā (fullness in arrival) · Location: Hands / lips / point of contact
Function: Closes the loop — desire reaches its object. The fulfilling power.
Somatic: The moment of contact: the cup meeting the lip, the gaze meeting the eye, the breath meeting the sky.
Etymology: Pra-āp (to reach, obtain) + ti — 'reaching, attainment.'
Phrase: Thank you for the reach that meets its object.
Iconography: Arm extended, fingertips just meeting a distant fruit, a hook in her hand. The power to attain what was far. The body knows her as the satisfying click of arrival — the thing finally in hand.

## Mātṛkās (11–18)

**11 · Brāhmī · ब्राह्मी**
Quality: Creation · Tattva: Vāc (speech / sacred utterance) · Location: Throat (the vowel-source) · Bīja: Aṃ — governs the A-row (16 vowels + anusvāra/visarga)
Function: Speech-as-creation. Every utterance begins with vowel-power. She is what makes voice possible.
Somatic: Open mouth. Held breath before sound. The column of voice rising from the chest.
Etymology: Brahma + ī (feminine suffix) — 'of Brahma,' the consort/shakti of the Creator.
Phrase: Thank you for the speech that begins.
Iconography: Four-faced, seated on a swan, rosary and water-pot in hand, the colour of dawn. The creative outpouring, the Word that begins worlds. First of the eight Mothers — she presides over the impulse to make.

**12 · Māheśvarī · माहेश्वरी**
Quality: Witness · Tattva: Ākāśa (space / sky) · Location: Throat (back / soft palate) · Bīja: Kaṃ — governs K-row
Function: K-row consonants form at the throat — the bridge between vowel and articulated speech.
Somatic: Back of the throat softening. Soft palate aware. The place where intention shapes itself into sound.
Etymology: Mahā (great) + Īśvarī (lord-feminine) — 'of the Great Lord (Shiva).'
Phrase: Thank you for the throat that holds sound.
Iconography: Three-eyed, riding the bull, trident in hand, a crescent in her hair, ash-pale. The still witness behind all change, Śiva's own power. She presides over the watching that does not interfere.

**13 · Kaumārī · कौमारी**
Quality: Fierce Youth · Tattva: Tejas (light / fire-element seed) · Location: Eyes / face / palate · Bīja: Caṃ — governs C-row
Function: Defensive-creative force. The protective shakti of the spiritual warrior.
Somatic: Alertness of the eyes. Readiness of the spine. The bright attention of the warrior at rest.
Etymology: Kumāra (youth, Skanda/Kārttikeya) + ī — 'of the youth,' the eternal-youth shakti.
Phrase: Thank you for the fierce youth that protects.
Iconography: Six-faced and ever-young, riding a peacock, a spear in hand, fresh as new flame. Undimmed first energy, the warrior-child. She presides over the unjaded ferocity of beginnings.

**14 · Vaiṣṇavī · वैष्णवी**
Quality: Preservation · Tattva: Sthiti (sustenance principle) · Location: Heart center / retroflex region · Bīja: Ṭaṃ — governs Ṭ-row
Function: The maintenance principle. What allows existence to continue.
Somatic: Steady rhythm of the heart. Unconscious continuation of breath. The body maintaining itself.
Etymology: Viṣṇu + ī — 'of Vishnu,' the sustainer-shakti.
Phrase: Thank you for the holding that sustains.
Iconography: Seated on Garuḍa, bearing conch, discus, mace and lotus, dark and serene. The sustaining power that holds creation in place. She presides over everything that keeps, maintains, and protects.

**15 · Vārāhī · वाराही**
Quality: Dissolution · Tattva: Bhūmi (earth principle) · Location: Belly / sacrum · Bīja: Taṃ — governs T-row
Function: The force that takes apart in order to reveal. Ego-dissolution.
Somatic: Loosening of holding patterns. Falling-away of unnecessary tension. The body softening into ground.
Etymology: Varāha (boar) + ī — 'boar-faced shakti,' the feminine of Vishnu's boar avatar.
Phrase: Thank you for the dissolving that integrates.
Iconography: Boar-faced, mounted on a buffalo, plough and pestle in hand, storm-dark. The power that breaks down and turns under, clearing the field. She presides over necessary destruction and fierce protection.

**16 · Indrāṇī · इन्द्राणी**
Quality: Heart-Sovereignty · Tattva: Indriya (the senses unified) · Location: Heart / chest / lips · Bīja: Paṃ — governs P-row
Function: Sovereignty over the celestial — the spaciousness of the open heart.
Somatic: Chest opening. Shoulders dropping back. The breath becoming spacious.
Etymology: Indra + āṇī — 'of Indra,' the king-of-gods shakti. Also called Aindrī.
Phrase: Thank you for the sovereignty of the heart.
Iconography: Thousand-eyed splendour on a great elephant, vajra in hand, jewel-bright. Royal power, the sovereignty of the heart's own domain. She presides over rightful rule and abundance.

**17 · Cāmuṇḍā · चामुण्डा**
Quality: Liberation · Tattva: Pralaya (final dissolution) · Location: Whole body / cremation-ground awareness · Bīja: Yaṃ — governs semivowels-sibilants (Y R L V Ś Ṣ S H)
Function: The destroyer of ignorance. Compassion at its most direct.
Somatic: Sudden cut-through of clarity. The moment a false thought is seen and dissolved instantly.
Etymology: Caṇḍa + Muṇḍa (two demons slain) — 'slayer of Canda and Munda,' a form of Kālī.
Phrase: Thank you for the fierce liberation that consumes illusion.
Iconography: Emaciated and terrible, seated on a corpse, skull-cup and severed head in hand, garlanded with skulls, laughing. The power that devours the ego and frees. She presides over the ferocious mercy that ends what must end.

**18 · Mahālakṣmī · महालक्ष्मी**
Quality: Wholeness · Tattva: Pūrṇa (fullness) · Location: Whole body unified · Bīja: Aim — the Sri-Vidya core syllable / all letters as totality
Function: The synthesis. The mother who is also each daughter. Not a separate matrika but the matrix itself.
Somatic: Full-body openness when all seven previous Matrikas are present at once.
Etymology: Mahā + Lakṣmī — 'Great Lakshmi,' the all-encompassing mother.
Phrase: Thank you for the wholeness that is all of them.
Iconography: Golden, four-armed, lotuses in two hands and the gestures of giving and fearlessness in the others, seated on a full-blown lotus. Integral abundance, the completion of the Mother-round. She presides over wholeness and grace.

## Mudrās — the living hand-seals (19–28)

# RING 2 — Sarvāśāparipūraka (16) — 16 petals · Sapphire on blood · The hour · Pelvis · per-Śakti tattva

*The home ring. Each Karṣiṇī carries her own tattva — this is where two rooms in one world diverge most. Cluster Groups share corridors.*

## Cluster: Inner Instrument (29–31)

**29 · Kāmākarṣiṇī · कामाकर्षिणी**
Quality: Kāma · Tattva: Pṛthivī (earth) · Bīja: aṃ · Location: Pelvic basin, gut, chest leaning forward
Somatic: The pull toward — a hunger, a longing, a leaning forward of the body. The magnetism that makes you reach for anything.
Etymology: Kāma (desire / wish / longing) + ākarṣiṇī (she who attracts) = She who attracts desire — the force that pulls wanting itself into existence. Before any specific want, there is the capacity to want; She is that capacity.
Phrase: Thank you for the desire that moves me toward life.
Iconography: Rosy-red, leaning forward with a flower-noose, drawing desire itself toward the center. She attracts the faculty of wanting — the very pull that first brought you to the lotus. The opening petal.

**30 · Buddhyākarṣiṇī · बुद्ध्याकर्षिणी**
Quality: Buddhi · Tattva: Ap/jala (water) · Bīja: āṃ · Location: Forehead, eyes
Somatic: The 'click' of understanding — a slight settling at the forehead/eyes, sometimes an audible 'aha' in the chest. The moment when formless longing becomes a clear sentence.
Etymology: Buddhi (intellect / discriminating awareness) + ākarṣiṇī = She who attracts intellect. The clarity by which one thing is distinguished from another — the faculty of discernment itself drawn into being.
Phrase: Thank you for the knowing that recognizes.
Iconography: Clear as morning, a small flame at her brow, drawing the intellect inward. She attracts understanding, gathering scattered comprehension into the single click of recognition.

**31 · Ahaṅkārākarṣiṇī · अहंकाराकर्षिणी**
Quality: Ahaṅkāra · Tattva: Tejas/agni (fire) · Bīja: iṃ · Location: Solar plexus, shoulders
Somatic: The contraction of 'mine'; the puffing-up of recognition; the heat that rises when status is touched.
Etymology: Ahaṅkāra (I-maker / ego-function) + ākarṣiṇī = She who attracts the I-making. Not vanity — the basic operation of consciousness identifying with itself as a separate self. The function before content.
Phrase: Thank you for the I that says I.
Iconography: Upright and self-possessed, a hand at her heart, drawing the I-sense toward its source. She attracts the ego — not to destroy it but to turn it home.

## Cluster: Tanmātra Sense Streams (32–36)

Iconography: Listening, one hand cupped to the ear, drawing sound and word inward. She attracts speech and the heard — the inner word before it reaches the throat.

**33 · Sparśākarṣiṇī · स्पर्शाकर्षिणी**

Iconography: Palm open and sensitive, drawing the whole field of touch toward the skin's awareness. She attracts contact — heat, cold, pressure — received as conversation.

**34 · Rūpākarṣiṇī · रूपाकर्षिणी**

**35 · Rasākarṣiṇī · रसाकर्षिणी**
Quality: Rasa · Tattva: Tvak (skin) · Bīja: ṛṃ · Location: Tongue, throat

Etymology: Rasa (taste / essence / juice / aesthetic flavor) + ākarṣiṇī = She who attracts taste — and beyond: aesthetic experience itself. Rasa carries double meaning: the taste of food and the taste of beauty. Both Her domain.
Phrase: Thank you for the taste that becomes savor.
Iconography: A nectar-cup at her lips, drawing taste and essence inward. She attracts flavour — the savour that makes an experience worth having and not merely had.

**36 · Gandhākarṣiṇī · गन्धाकर्षिणी**
Quality: Gandha · Tattva: Cakṣus (eye) · Bīja: ṝṃ · Location: Bridge of nose
Somatic: The nostrils flaring; the involuntary inhalation near something fragrant; the way certain incenses (sandalwood, jasmine) immediately quiet the mind.
Etymology: Gandha (smell / scent / fragrance) + ākarṣiṇī = She who attracts smell. The tanmatra of olfaction — the subtlest of the elemental tanmatras, the one most directly tied to memory and the earth element.
Phrase: Thank you for the scent that becomes memory.
Iconography: Holding a lotus to her face, drawing scent inward through the breath. She attracts smell — the oldest sense, reaching memory beneath thought.

## Cluster: Stability Powers (38–41)

**38 · Dhairyākarṣiṇī · धैर्याकर्षिणी**
Quality: Dhairya · Tattva: Ghrāṇa (nose) · Bīja: ḹṃ · Location: Back-body, spine
Somatic: The lengthening of the spine; the steadying of the diaphragm; the inner 'I will stay' that arrives before the thought of staying. Quieting of the jaw.
Etymology: Dhairya (courage / steadiness / fortitude) + ākarṣiṇī = She who attracts courage. Cosmologically essential — without the capacity to stand under experience, no other Shakti's work completes. Load-bearing at the level of reality itself.
Phrase: Thank you for the courage to hold ground.
Iconography: Spine long and unmoving, drawing steadiness inward. She attracts fortitude — the quiet "I will stay" that holds when everything argues for leaving.

**39 · Smṛtyākarṣiṇī · स्मृत्याकर्षिणी**
Quality: Smṛti · Tattva: Vāk (speech) · Bīja: eṃ · Location: Eyes turning inward
Somatic: The slight inward turning of the gaze when something is recalled; the body orienting before conscious recall — you turn toward the door before you consciously hear the knock.
Etymology: Smṛti (memory / mindfulness / recollection) + ākarṣiṇī = She who attracts memory. Both ordinary memory (what happened) and yogic memory (what is). Without Her, no continuity — each moment would be unrelated to the last.
Phrase: Thank you for the memory that returns me to myself.
Iconography: Eyes half-turned inward, drawing memory toward the surface. She attracts recollection — the unforgetting that returns a whole season of life in a single phrase.

**40 · Nāmākarṣiṇī · नामाकर्षिणी**
Quality: Nāma · Tattva: Pāṇi (hand) · Bīja: aiṃ · Location: Throat, whole body settling
Somatic: The instant a name surfaces and the body slightly relaxes — the 'of course.' The strange satisfaction of finally remembering what something is called.
Etymology: Nāma (name / designation / linguistic identity) + ākarṣiṇī = She who attracts name. The form by which the formless lets itself be addressed. The dharmic frequency that makes a self callable.
Phrase: Thank you for the name that lets me be called.
Iconography: Speaking a silent syllable, drawing each thing toward its true name. She attracts naming — calling every being into its own dharmic note.

**41 · Bījākarṣiṇī · बीजाकर्षिणी**
Quality: Bīja · Tattva: Pāda (foot) · Bīja: oṃ · Location: Deep belly
Somatic: The contraction-and-release that signals creative emergence; the deep-belly quickening before an idea forms; the feeling of 'this is the beginning of something.'
Etymology: Bīja (seed / origin / mantric syllable) + ākarṣiṇī = She who attracts seed. The compressed potential before anything has unfolded. The bridge between named self and Self — what is small enough to be a point and total enough to become a universe.
Phrase: Thank you for the seed that becomes everything.
Iconography: Hands cupped around a glowing seed at the belly, drawing potential toward beginning. She attracts the seed-essence — the quickening just before an idea forms.

## Cluster: Self-Body-Immortality (42–44)

**42 · Ātmākarṣiṇī · आत्माकर्षिणी**
Quality: Ātman · Tattva: Pāyu (anus — release/elimination). Self-recognition comes through release. The karmendriya of letting go. · Bīja: auṃ · Location: Body's centre
Somatic: The instant of 'oh, I am here' — a sudden settling into the body's centre; the relief of remembering you are the one to whom all this is happening.
Etymology: Ātmā (Self / soul / the witness) + ākarṣiṇī = She who attracts the Self. The pull toward the awareness behind every awareness. What watches without being watched.
Phrase: Thank you for the Self that witnesses.
Iconography: Luminous and inward-turned, drawing the sense of "I" toward the witness behind it. She attracts the Self — the one to whom all of this is happening.

**43 · Amṛtākarṣiṇī · अमृताकर्षिणी**
Quality: Amṛta · Tattva: Upastha (genitals — generation). The procreative power and the nectar of immortality are both 'fluids of continuance.' · Bīja: aṃ — anusvāra · Location: Soft palate, crown
Somatic: The cool sweetness at the back of the throat during deep meditation; the post-prayer hum at the crown; the felt-sense of being inwardly fed without having eaten.
Etymology: Amṛta (deathless / immortal / nectar) + ākarṣiṇī = She who attracts the deathless. The pull toward what does not pass. Not denial of death but recognition of what remains when bodies do not.
Phrase: Thank you for the deathless that remains.
Iconography: A drop of nectar at the crown, drawing the deathless sweetness downward through the body. She attracts immortality — the cool sweetness of deep meditation.

**44 · Śarīrākarṣiṇī · शरीराकर्षिणी**
Quality: Śarīra · Tattva: Manas (mind). THE KEY PAIRING: body and mind placed at the exact same point. The deepest physicality and the most subtle psyche are not two different things — one movement arriving from two directions. · Bīja: aḥ — visarga · Location: Whole body, ground
Somatic: The simple weight of sitting; the breath landing in the belly; the sensation of 'I am here, in this body, this much.' Often felt as a gentle dropping or grounding.
Etymology: Śarīra (body / vessel / form) + ākarṣiṇī = She who attracts the body. The closing of the 16-petal lotus. Her esoteric tattva is manas (mind) — the tantric declaration that the deepest physicality and the most subtle psyche are not two.
Phrase: Thank you for the body that holds the soul.
Iconography: Grounded, both palms resting on the body, drawing all the foregoing back into flesh. She attracts the body itself — where the inward journey turns out to have been a journey home. She closes the ring.
