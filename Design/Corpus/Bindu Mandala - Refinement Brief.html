Part IThe five points, explained

These are the five calls from the audit. None are bugs — they're decisions the app has drifted into without anyone choosing them on purpose. Here's what each one means, why it matters, and the single decision it asks of you.

1

The daily spine, versus the museum

The app was designed around one small daily loop: open it, meet today's Śakti, make a gesture of recognition, close it. That loop was the home screen — the first thing you saw. In the build today, you land on the Mandala instead, and "Today" is the second item inside a menu you have to open. The app has also grown six destinations and nine worlds to wander through.

Each of those is lovely. But together they quietly change what the app is — from a daily intimacy you return to, into a place you browse when you happen to think of it. The most habit-forming, most personal surface is now the hardest one to reach.

The decision

Choose, deliberately, what opening the app puts in front of you — her (today's Śakti and the gesture) or the whole cosmos (the Mandala). Both are valid. One idea: keep the Mandala as home, but let today's pull always be visible on it and tap straight through to her — so the daily rite survives even inside the bigger world.

2

The status pill lost its ceremony

Originally, a Śakti's status — mapped → exploring → active → embodied — advanced when you tapped the pill and chose to deepen the relationship. It was meant to feel like a held breath: a small, conscious rite. In the build, status now advances on its own, once you've recognized her enough times.

That automatic version may genuinely be better — it's earned, not declared. The problem is only that the pill still looks like a button (it has the capsule outline of something tappable) but does nothing when pressed. It promises an action it no longer delivers.

The decision

Pick one: strip the button styling so it plainly reads as a state she has reached — or give the practitioner back a deliberate gesture for crossing the threshold. Just don't leave it dressed as a control that does nothing.

3

The detail screen is becoming an encyclopedia

The screen you reach by tapping a petal — her reading — has grown to roughly twelve stacked sections: name, quality, somatic line, appreciation, bīja, iconography, codex portrait, lineage, cosmic function, tattva, field, moments. The codex portrait is described in your own code as "the soul of the screen" — yet it sits seventh, given no more weight than the scholarly reference around it.

The result reads like a wiki entry rather than a devotion. Everything is present; nothing is foregrounded.

The decision

Build a hierarchy. Lead with the soul — the portrait and the somatic line — and tuck the reference matter (lineage, cosmic function, tattva, iconography) behind a single "go deeper" disclosure. Let this screen breathe like the worlds do.

4

Two hidden doors, and no signpost

All navigation lives in two concealed places: the hamburger at the top-right (three faint gold lines at under half-opacity), and the Veil you pull up from the bottom. The Veil at least breathes a chevron so you know it's there. The hamburger gives no hint at all — yet it is the only path to Today, the Well, the 102, Memory, and Settings.

A first-time hand resting on the Mandala may simply never discover half the app exists. Intimate restraint is right; total invisibility is one step past it.

The decision

Give the hamburger the same courtesy the Veil already has — a single faint first-run breath, or a one-time reveal on launch — so the door is found once, then forgotten. It never has to shout; it just has to be seen the first time.

5

The legibility floor

The instinct — barely-there text for a settled, contemplative eye — is good and worth protecting. But several elements have crossed from quiet into genuinely unreadable. The most important offender: the home lotus petal labels, at roughly 7.5pt and 38% opacity — that's the type naming your primary navigation. Others: the Silence name-stars (10–11pt at 11–16% opacity), the Ring 1 hint line, assorted tracked captions.

The decision

For each faint element, decide one word: label or texture. If it's texture — atmosphere never meant to be read — leave it exactly as is. If it's meant to be read, lift it to at least 11pt and 30% opacity. The petal labels especially deserve a real decision, not an accident of scale.

Part I is yours to decide. Part II is just broken, and can be fixed today.

Part IIThe five rings you walked

Two threads run through everything you found: the energies are too small to touch (most use 26–32pt tap areas; iOS wants 44pt minimum), and the figures sit small in a tall screen with a lot of dead space. The good news — fixing both at once is the same move: enlarge each energy's hit area to ≥44pt and scale the ring figures up to fill more of the screen. Bigger figures spread the energies apart, which makes them easier to hit for free. Below, each ring's exact cause and fix.

Ring 1

The Earth-City — hard to touch the energies touch

What you felt

The 28 lights are very hard to land on — the tap area is too precise.

Root cause · RingOneWorldView.swift

Each light is built with .frame(width: 26, height: 26) and .contentShape(Circle()) — a 26pt target, well under the 44pt minimum. And 28 of them are packed onto three nested squares; the inner square is only ~144pt wide but carries 10 lights, so the targets are both tiny and crowded.

Fix

Grow each light's invisible hit area to ≥44pt while keeping the visible dot small — only the contentShape frame grows. Because 28 × 44pt would overlap, also enlarge the whole figure (raise the square half-sizes 150 / 110 / 72 and the breathing room) so the lights spread out. If crowding remains, resolve a tap to the nearest light within range rather than requiring a direct hit.

Ring 3

The Smoke Lotus — a poor version of the craft rendering

What you felt

The current image feels like a much weaker version of what was originally crafted.

Root cause · three regressions from ring-worlds-3.jsx → RingThreeWorldView.swift

1 · The glow died. The original lit each plume with CSS filter: brightness(1.5) — a true multiply of light. The Swift port uses .brightness(0.15), which only adds a faint amount. So the plumes never light up; active and resting look nearly the same, flat and dim.
2 · The air went still. The original carried DustMotes count=7 drifting through the scene. The Swift Ring 3 has no dust motes at all — the atmosphere is gone.
3 · The smoke reads muddy. The warm gradient stops at endRadius: 92 but the plume shape reaches outerR: 116, so the tip fades to nothing; combined with the dead brightness it looks like a blurred shape, not glowing smoke.

Fix

Restore the glow with a real luminance lift — raise the gradient's warm-stop opacity and layer a soft white-warm overlay on the active/unified plume (not .brightness()), matching the original ladder: active brightest → scattered dimmest. Add DustMotesView(count: 7). Extend the gradient to the plume tip so the smoke holds its shape.

// the regression — additive, barely visible let brightness: Double = isActive ? 0.15 : (gather ? 0.05 : -0.10) .brightness(brightness) // restore a true glow: drive opacity + a warm overlay instead let glow: Double = isActive ? 1.0 : unified ? 0.9 : gather ? 0.55 : 0.30 // raise gradient warm-stop to ~0.95, add .blendMode(.screen) overlay, // and add DustMotesView(count: 7) to the ZStack
Ring 6

The Concealed — can't find the shaktis touch

What you felt

Very hard to find and tap the shaktis — the tap area is far too precise.

Root cause · RingSixWorldView.swift

A double-precision trap. Each triangle is a .frame(width: 32, height: 32) target (under 44pt), and tapping is gated by guard distance <= revealRadius — you can only name what the moving light has already reached. Since the light follows your finger, the target is small and conditional and nearly invisible until the light is right on it. Three things fighting you at once.

Fix

Grow each triangle's contentShape to ≥44pt; widen the orbit (currently radius 96) so the ten spread apart; and relax the gate — let a tap land on anything currently lit, or grow revealRadius, so the user isn't also fighting the light's reach. The concealment should be poetic, not a precision test.

Ring 8

The Vāk Chamber — four names superimposed layout bug

What you felt

Multiple names stacked on the right — like 8 originals, with 4 more laid on top of some of them instead of added outside the structure.

Root cause · RingSevenWorldView.swift (Sarvarogahara · the Vāk Chamber)

Exactly right, and exactly findable. The chamber was extended from 8 voices to 12 (the code even comments "extended from Phase 3's eight"), but the angular spacing was left at 45°: theta = i * 45 - 90. Since 360° ÷ 45° = exactly 8 positions, voices 9–12 — Vāṅmayī, Onmādinī, Mantreśī, Śaktimayī — land precisely on top of voices 1–4. Those are your four superimposed names.

Fix

Two clean options. Simplest: space all twelve evenly at 30°. Or — matching your instinct that the new four should sit "outside the current structure" — keep eight on the inner orbit and place the extra four on a larger outer ring.

// 12 voices at 45° spacing → 4 collide with the first 4 let theta = (Double(i) * 45 - 90) * .pi / 180 // even twelve around the circle (and widen orbitR a touch) let theta = (Double(i) * 30 - 90) * .pi / 180

Note on the number: this file is RingSeven internally, but its content (the Vāk syllables, "12 voices sounded") is unmistakably the chamber you described as Ring 8 — worth confirming the world↔number mapping while you're in here, since the stale comment // 8 Vāk circles now iterates twelve.

Ring 9

The Descent — a stray field at the end content

What you felt

When you hold to the end, an Airtable field appears — too long, and it takes away from the experience.

Root cause · RingNineDescentView.swift

In the recognition caption, once you hold past the threshold (r > 0.6), the view appends the avaraṇa's Airtable personalConnection field beneath the benediction. It was meant as a quiet undertone, but the field's length breaks the stillness of the arrival.

Fix

Remove that block entirely. The descent should land on "You did not arrive. You were the arriving." and the single gold benediction line — and then nothing. The silence after is the point.

In one breath

The rings are the easy part — four are small, exact bugs and one is a rendering regression, all fixable this week. Yes, I can see all of it through the code — the superimposed Vāk names, the dead brightness in the smoke, the 26-point targets, the stray field. The five points are the harder, better questions: they're about what the app is becoming as it grows, and those are yours to answer. Fix the rings, then let's sit with the five.
