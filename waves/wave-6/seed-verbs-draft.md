## Hesitations

- **Ahaṅkārākarṣiṇī: claims.** It comes from her own row ("ahaṃkāra claims"), but it could read as a judgment of the ego, which the row says is "to be witnessed, not destroyed". The other option is "identifies".
- **Mahātripurasundarī: contains**, set against **Mahālakṣmī: encompasses**. The two say nearly the same thing, and that sameness is real in the rows. "Plays" (Lalitā) would fit the Source, but her row never says it, so I left it out.
- **The three sovereignty verbs: Īśitva rules, Indrāṇī reigns, Sarveśī governs.** All three are distinct verbs for one shared idea. Indrāṇī could instead take something from "the spaciousness of the open heart".
- **The two "will" verbs: Prākāmya wills, Kāmeśvarī desires**, with **Icchā wishes**. These three are close neighbours and are told apart only by the root each name holds.
- **The Mudrā and Devī pairs share one name, so the Devī got the second-truest verb.** This covers stirs/agitates, liquefies/melts, attracts/draws, attunes/aligns and enraptures/intoxicates. The Mudrā took the literal translation, except for Vaśaṅkarī, where each verb came from that row's own Function.
- **The three hooks: Mahāṅkuśā steers, Anaṅga-Aṅkuśā recalls, Aṅkuśinī goads.** Aṅkuśinī kept "goads" because she is literally "the goad-bearer". The other two verbs come from each one's Function.
- **Vaśinī: commands.** The name gives "commands", while her Function ("pre-articulate vibration") would give "vibrates".
- **Kāmeśī: woos.** This is exact for speech that carries longing, but it is the most coloured verb in the set. The plainer option is "conveys".
- **Kāmākarṣiṇī: kindles.** This is a slight reach past "draws desire". The alternative is "yearns".
- **Sarva-Jṛmbhiṇī: stretches.** The literal word is "yawns".
- **Sarvajñānamayī: reveals.** Several Nigarbha Functions open with "Reveals…", and she kept it because her row is the only one where the revealing is about knowing. The alternative is "illumines".
- **The Karshinis.** All sixteen are "she who attracts X", so "draws" stayed with the Ākarṣiṇī Mudrā. Each Karshini's verb is the act of her own object (discerns, hears, touches, sees, savors, smells, remembers, names and so on), taken from her Function phrase.
